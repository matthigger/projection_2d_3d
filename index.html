Projecting onto a line

All multiples ca form a line, the span of a. The projection p is the point on that line closest to b.

At the closest point, the error e = b − p meets the line at a right angle:

a · (b − ca) = 0 ⇒ c = a·ba·a,   p = ca

Try this

  • Drag b onto the dashed line: e shrinks to zero and p = b.
  • Make b perpendicular to a: the projection is 0.
  • Stretch or flip a without changing its line: p stays put and only c changes. The projection depends on the span, not on a itself.

Least squares: with one feature and no intercept, a is the feature column x, b is the target y, and c = x·y / x·x is the fitted slope β̂.

Projecting onto a plane

a₁ and a₂ span a plane. Put them side by side as the columns of A = [a₁ a₂]. Every point in the plane is then Ac = c₁a₁ + c₂a₂.

The closest point p = Ac leaves an error e = b − p perpendicular to the whole plane, so to both a₁ and a₂:

AT(b − Ac) = 0 ⇒ ATA c = ATb

These are the normal equations. Solving them gives c = (ATA)−1ATb.

Try this

  • Click Edge-on to look along the plane: the right angle between e and the plane is plain to see.
  • Drag the tip of b into the plane: e vanishes.
  • Click a₂ ← a₁ + a₂. The plane is the same, so p doesn't move, but c₁ and c₂ change.

Least squares: A is the design matrix X (one column per feature), b is y, c is β̂, p is the fit ŷ, and e is the residual. With n samples the same picture lives in ℝn; here n = 3, so we can look at it.
